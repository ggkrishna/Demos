📖 Quick Reference: Clinical Decision Support Systems (CDSS)

A Clinical Decision Support System (CDSS) is a health information technology system designed to provide physicians, nurses, and other health professionals with clinical decision support—assisting with clinical decision-making tasks at the point of care.

1. Three Core Components

  • Knowledge Base: Compiled clinical rules, guidelines, and drug interaction databases (IF-THEN logic).
  • Inference Engine: The processor that combines the rules in the Knowledge Base with actual patient data from the EHR.
  • Communication Mechanism: How data enters the system and how alerts/recommendations are displayed to the clinician.

2. Types of CDSS

  • Knowledge-Based (Shown Below): Uses explicit programmed IF-THEN rules created by medical experts.
  • Non-Knowledge-Based: Uses Artificial Intelligence (AI) or Machine Learning (ML) to find patterns in historical clinical data without hard-coded rules.

3. The "Five Rights" of CDSS

To be effective, a CDSS must deliver:
  1. The right information
  2. To the right person
  3. In the right intervention format
  4. Through the right channel
  5. At the right time in workflow

4. Key Challenges in Informatics

  • Alert Fatigue: Too many insignificant pop-ups cause clinicians to ignore or override critical alerts.
  • Workflow Disruption: Poorly timed alerts slow down patient care.
  • Rule Maintenance: Medical guidelines change frequently, requiring constant updates to the Knowledge Base.

Rule 1B (Critical Hyperpyrexia):
IF Temp ≥ 40.0 → Medical Emergency Alert
Rule 2 (Tachycardia):
IF Heart Rate > 90 bpm → Flag Tachycardia
Rule 3 (Abnormal WBC):
IF WBC > 12.0 OR WBC < 4.0 → Flag Leukocytosis
Rule 4 (Multi-Parameter Sepsis Check):
IF ≥ 2 Vital Rules (1A, 2, 3) are TRUE → Sepsis Alert
Rule 5 (Drug-Allergy Check):
